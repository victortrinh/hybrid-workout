# HYROX training: what the evidence says

Research note for the 24-week Program (Mon 9 Nov 2026 → Race day Thu 22 Apr 2027, HYROX Doubles Men Open trained solo at singles volume). Budget: four required Sessions per Week of ≤45 min (two HYROX Sessions, one Threshold/Speed Session, one Strength Session) plus 1–2 optional Easy runs; three Race simulations of 60–85 min are exempt from the cap. Stations are always at Race load; progression is Station volume, then time targets; lifts progress by load.

Every claim below is tied to the source that owns it. Where only coaching practice or HYROX-brand material exists, it is labelled **coaching lore, not evidence**. Searched 7 Nov 2026.

Vocabulary follows `CONTEXT.md` (Session, Week, Station, Race load, Training load, Deload week, Taper, Race simulation).

---

## 1. What determines HYROX Finish time

The peer-reviewed HYROX literature is small (2025–2026) and mostly observational. Four primary papers and two reviews were found.

**Race format and Race load** (brand source, used only for the format facts): 8 × 1 km run, each followed by a Station; Men's Open loads are SkiErg 1000 m, sled push 50 m at 152 kg incl. sled, sled pull 50 m at 103 kg incl. sled, burpee broad jump 80 m, row 1000 m, farmers carry 200 m with 2 × 24 kg, sandbag lunges 100 m with 20 kg, wall balls 100 reps with 6 kg. The HYROX page makes no claim about what share of the race is running. https://hyrox.com/the-fitness-race/

**Run share of Finish time**
- Brandt et al. 2025, *Frontiers in Physiology* (n = 11 recreational athletes, simulated race on a treadmill, alone, non-official sled): total 86.5 (14.5) min; runs 51.2 (14.1) min vs Stations 32.8 (6.1) min (p = 0.003), i.e. runs ≈ 59% of the clock in this slow field. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240
- Rappelt et al. 2026, *Frontiers in Physiology* (all official PRO and ELITE results, seasons 1–7, 27,854 male and 11,842 female results after filtering): running "consistently accounted for ~50% of total race time"; Top-100 men 50.9% in season 1 → 48.5% in season 7. Absolute running time in the top 100 fell from 35:44 to 27:38 (men). DOI 10.3389/fphys.2026.1847569
- Reading: faster fields have a *smaller* run share because stations compress less than runs do. For a recreational Men's Open finisher the run share is likely 55–60%.

**Which Stations correlate most with Finish time**
- Rappelt et al. 2026 (quantile regression, pseudo-R² of each discipline's time against remaining race time, men): rowing 0.25–0.46 highest, then sandbag lunges 0.19–0.39, burpee broad jump 0.18–0.36, SkiErg 0.19–0.32, running 0.14–0.35. Coefficients grew at higher (slower) quantiles, so slower athletes lose more total time per unit of Station time, with the strongest quantile-dependent effects in the strength-based Stations (largest shift: sled pull in men, Δβ = +402 s, season 7). Caveat: PRO/ELITE fields only, and total running time had the lowest rank reshuffling across seasons (running is the most stable discriminator). DOI 10.3389/fphys.2026.1847569
- Brandt et al. 2025: Finish time correlated with VO2max (ρ = −0.71, p = 0.01), endurance training volume (ρ = −0.68, p = 0.04) and body-fat % (ρ = 0.67, p = 0.03). Not significant: hand-grip strength (ρ = 0.09), muscle mass %, body mass, BMI, resistance-training volume. Station time showed no significant correlations with any fitness variable in this n = 11 sample. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240
- Fastest Stations in that sample were the heaviest ones: sled push 2.1 (0.6) min, sled pull 2.6 (0.6) min, farmers carry 2.7 (0.7) min. Peak HR, lactate and RPE all occurred at wall balls (lactate 8.5 (4.9) mmol/L). Blood lactate was higher on Stations than on runs (8.5 vs 7.7 mmol/L, p = 0.006); 79.5% of race time was spent at "very hard" HR intensity. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240

**"Compromised running" (slowdown vs open running)**
- Gutiérrez-Hellín et al. 2026, *IJSPP*, HYScience study (24 PRO men, lab VT2/vVO2max plus HYROX Madrid 2025 splits): running speed fell from ≈16.1 km/h on run 1 to ≈13.6 km/h on run 8 (−15%); mean intensity ≈84% of VT2 speed and 76% of vVO2max; "sustaining a higher relative intensity appears central to shorter total running time". DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2026-0098 (abstract via Europe PMC, PMID 42697540; full text paywalled)
- Brandt et al. 2025: longest runs were run 5 (7.4 (1.6) min), run 8 (6.8 (1.3)) and run 6 (6.8 (1.9)), consistent with slowing after sled push/pull, but per-run pacing was not analysed. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240
- Rappelt et al. 2026 could not analyse per-run pacing because course distances varied between events. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2026.1847569
- No peer-reviewed paper quantifies HYROX run pace against the same athlete's open 5K or 1 km pace. The "compromised running is X% slower than open running" figures circulating online are **coaching lore, not evidence** (commercial results dashboards such as HyroxDataLab/RoxCoach are unaudited and not peer reviewed).

**Reviews**
- Davids 2026, *Strength and Conditioning Journal* 48(1):88–100: narrative review naming aerobic capacity, anaerobic power, local muscular endurance and maximal strength as determinants; no quantitative data of its own. DOI 10.1519/SSC.0000000000000913
- "Hyrox®: Current Evidence on Physiology, Psychology and Performance Determinants" (Preprints.org, July 2026, 11 studies): "running capacity appears to be the strongest determinant"; evidence base "mostly observational or based on public competition data". Not peer reviewed. https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202607.1912
- A single-athlete case study of a HYROX world champion exists (German Journal of Exercise and Sport Research, 2026, DOI 10.1007/s12662-026-01162-0); abstract not accessible during this search, and n = 1 is not generalisable.

## 2. Interval and threshold prescriptions for repeated ~1 km efforts

**Long-interval HIIT raises VO2max more than threshold or easy running at matched work**
- Helgerud et al. 2007, *MSSE* (40 moderately trained men, 3 d/wk × 8 wk, four protocols matched for total O2 cost): 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax with 3 min at 70% HRmax raised VO2max 7.2% (55.5 → 60.4 mL/kg/min); 15 s/15 s raised it 5.5%; long slow distance (70% HRmax) and lactate-threshold running (85% HRmax) did not raise VO2max significantly (p < 0.01 for HIIT vs both). Stroke volume rose ≈10% after interval training only. DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570
- Milanović, Sporiš & Weston 2015, *Sports Medicine* (28 controlled trials, healthy adults 18–45): HIIT vs no exercise +5.5 mL/kg/min (±1.2); endurance training vs no exercise +4.9 (±1.4); HIIT vs endurance training +1.2 (±0.9), "possibly small"; longer HIIT repetitions gave small additional gains. DOI 10.1007/s40279-015-0365-0
- Wen et al. 2019, *J Sci Med Sport* (53 RCTs): vs control, even short intervals (≤30 s), low volume (≤5 min/session) and short programs (≤4 wk) raised VO2max; vs moderate continuous training, only long intervals (≥2 min), high volume (≥15 min of work per session) and ≥4–12 wk programs were superior. DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.01.013

**Polarised (hard/easy) beats threshold-heavy distribution at equal load in trained and recreational runners**
- Stöggl & Sperlich 2014, *Frontiers in Physiology* (48 trained endurance athletes, 9 wk, HR-controlled): polarised group gained VO2peak +11.7% (+6.8 mL/kg/min, p < 0.001), time to exhaustion +17.4%, peak velocity +5.1%; threshold and high-volume groups did not improve performance variables; HIIT group improved v4mmol +5.6%. No group difference in work economy. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2014.00033
- Muñoz et al. 2014, *IJSPP* (30 recreational runners, 10 wk): polarised (77/3/20% in zones 1/2/3) improved 10 km 5.0% (39:18 → 37:19) vs between-thresholds (46/35/19%) 3.6% (39:24 → 38:00); the ≈41 s difference was not significant overall; a subset analysis favoured polarised. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2012-0350
- Rosenblat, Perrotta & Vicenzino 2019, *JSCR* meta-analysis (3 RCTs pooled): time-trial effect favouring polarised over threshold ES = −0.66 (95% CI −1.17 to −0.15); PEDro 4–5/10. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000002618
- Silva Oliveira, Boppre & Fonseca 2024, *Sports Medicine* (17 studies, 437 participants): polarised vs all other distributions, VO2peak SMD 0.24 (0.01–0.48), p = 0.04; time trial SMD −0.01 (−0.28 to 0.25), p = 0.92; the VO2peak advantage appeared only in programs < 12 wk (SMD 0.40) and in highly trained athletes (SMD 0.46); polarised vs pyramidal: no difference on any outcome (VO2peak SMD 0.08, TT −0.07). DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z
- Seiler 2010, *IJSPP* review: elite endurance athletes training 10–13 times/wk converge on ≈80% low intensity; intensification studies in already well-trained athletes show no convincing long-term gain from more HIIT. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276

**Minimum effective dose**
- Helgerud 2007 used 3 HIIT sessions/wk; Stöggl & Sperlich's polarised group did about 2 hard sessions/wk within ≈20% of volume; Muñoz 2014 had 20% of load in zone 3. García-Pinillos et al. 2017 review of HIIT in endurance runners (23 studies): interventions used 2–4 HIIT sessions/wk, ≥4 wk, mostly alongside continuous runs; both HIIT and continuous running should be included. DOI 10.1016/j.jshs.2016.08.010
- Wen 2019 gives the per-session floor for beating continuous training: ≥2 min intervals, ≥15 min of hard work per session. No study isolates one HIIT session per week in runners against two. Within this Program the two HYROX Sessions are themselves repeated ~1 km efforts at "very hard" HR (Brandt 2025), so the athlete's weekly hard-interval dose is already 2–3 sessions before the Threshold Session is counted.

**Specific to 8 × 1 km with work between**: no controlled study of interval running interrupted by loaded work was found. The closest evidence is acute: a single bout of resistance exercise impairs subsequent running economy and time-to-exhaustion for hours to days (Doma, Deakin & Bentley 2017, *Sports Medicine*, DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0758-3). Training the run-after-Station pattern is **coaching lore, not evidence**, though it follows from specificity and from the HYScience 15% pace decline.

## 3. Strength training: transfer and concurrent-training interference

**Strength training improves running economy and time-trial performance**
- Balsalobre-Fernández, Santos-Concejero & Grivas 2016, *JSCR* (5 studies, 93 competitive runners): running economy SMD −1.42 (95% CI −2.23 to −0.60); 4 of 5 programs used 40–70% 1RM; recommended 2–3 sessions/wk for 8–12 wk mixing resistance and plyometrics. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001316
- Blagrove, Howatson & Hayes 2018, *Sports Medicine* 48(5):1117–1149 (24 studies): running economy improved 2–8% vs control in most of 20 studies; time-trial (1.5–10 km) and sprint performance also tended to improve; VO2max, vVO2max, lactate and body composition typically unchanged; 2–3 ST sessions/wk suggested. DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0835-7
- Llanos-Lagos et al. 2024, *Sports Medicine* 54(4):895–932 (three-level meta-analysis, 652 runners, programs 6–24 wk, 1–4 sessions/wk): high-load training improved running economy at 8.6–17.9 km/h, ES = −0.266 (p = 0.039); combined methods ES = −0.426 (p = 0.018); plyometrics only at ≤12 km/h (small); submaximal-load and isometric training did not. Certainty moderate for high-load, low for combined. DOI 10.1007/s40279-023-01978-y

**Transfer to sled push/pull and loaded carries**
- No training study measures transfer of gym strength to HYROX sled or carry Station times. The nearest literature is resisted-sled sprint training: Alcaraz et al. 2018, *Sports Medicine* 48(9):2143–2165 (13 studies) found improvement in acceleration-phase sprinting (SMD 0.57) but not more than unresisted sprinting, and load did not determine the gain; volume of >160 m/session, 2–3 sessions/wk was recommended. DOI 10.1007/s40279-018-0947-8. This is sprint transfer, not a 152 kg push for 50 m.
- Brandt 2025 found no correlation between grip strength, muscle mass or resistance-training volume and Finish time in recreational athletes (n = 11), while Rappelt 2026 found sled pull and sandbag lunges carry the largest penalties for slower PRO athletes. Together these say: for a Men's Open athlete the Stations must not be a weakness, but marginal strength beyond that buys little. Specific transfer of farmers-carry or lunge training to HYROX splits: **coaching lore, not evidence**.

**Interference effect**
- Wilson et al. 2012, *JSCR* 26(8):2293–2307 (21 studies, 422 effect sizes): strength ES 1.76 (ST only) vs 1.44 (concurrent); hypertrophy 1.23 vs 0.85; power 0.91 vs 0.55 (all three groups differ for power). Running, but not cycling, produced significant decrements in hypertrophy and strength. Negative correlations with endurance frequency (−0.26 to −0.35) and session duration (−0.29 to −0.75). DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31823a3e2d
- Schumann et al. 2022, *Sports Medicine* 52(3):601–612 (43 studies): concurrent vs strength-only, hypertrophy SMD −0.01 (p = 0.92), maximal strength SMD −0.06 (p = 0.45), explosive strength SMD −0.28 (−0.48 to −0.08; p = 0.007); explosive-strength attenuation was more pronounced when both modes were in the same session (p = 0.043) than when separated by ≥3 h; independent of aerobic mode, frequency, training status and age. DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7
- Eddens, van Someren & Howatson 2018, *Sports Medicine* 48(1):177–188 (10 studies, ≥5 wk): resistance-then-endurance order favoured lower-body dynamic strength, weighted mean difference 6.91%; no order effect on static strength, hypertrophy, VO2max or body fat. DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1
- Doma, Deakin & Bentley 2017, *Sports Medicine* 47(11):2187–2200: a single resistance bout leaves residual fatigue that can impair endurance-session quality for "several hours to days"; manage with mode order, between-mode recovery and intensity. DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0758-3

**Scheduling within 4 sessions/wk**: the evidence supports (a) strength and running on separate days or ≥3 h apart; (b) if same day, lift first; (c) protecting the Threshold Session from the day after the Strength Session; (d) accepting that explosive strength, not maximal strength, is what the running will cost.

## 4. Deload frequency and Taper

**Deload weeks**
- Bell et al. 2023, *Sports Medicine – Open* 9:87, Delphi consensus of 21–34 strength coaches: 100% agreed there are limited scientific studies on deloading and current practice rests on coach experience; "every 4–6 weeks" and "~7 days" come from background literature, not panel consensus; consensus that deloads may be pre-planned (100%), autoregulated (100%) or both (100%); volume may drop while intensity stays the same (95.2%); intensity may remain high (81%). DOI 10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0
- Bell et al. 2022, *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, coach survey: most common deload duration is one week; volume and intensity are what coaches cut. DOI 10.3389/fspor.2022.1073223
- Coleman et al. 2024, *PeerJ* 12:e16777 (RCT, n = 39 resistance-trained, 9 wk): a full one-week break at the midpoint produced no difference in hypertrophy, local endurance or power, but the continuously training group gained more isometric and dynamic lower-body strength. DOI 10.7717/peerj.16777. A complete break is costlier than a reduced week; the Program's "reduce by about a third" Deload week is not the condition tested.
- Aubry et al. 2014, *MSSE* 46(9):1769–1777 (triathletes, 3 wk overload then taper): functionally overreached athletes had *smaller* post-taper supercompensation than acutely fatigued athletes (F-OR 2.6% ± 1.1% vs control 2.6% ± 1.6%; AF small-to-large greater). Pushing into overreaching before a recovery period does not pay. DOI 10.1249/mss.0000000000000301
- Planned deload every 4–5 weeks vs autoregulation: no controlled comparison exists in either strength or endurance populations. "Every 4th week" is **coaching lore, not evidence**; the Bell consensus explicitly allows either.

**Taper**
- Bosquet, Montpetit, Arvisais & Mujika 2007, *MSSE* 39(8):1358–1365 (27 studies): overall taper effect for 2-wk duration 0.59 ± 0.33; exponential volume reduction of 41–60% gave 0.72 ± 0.36; unchanged intensity 0.33 ± 0.14; unchanged frequency 0.35 ± 0.17 (all p < 0.001). Recommendation: 2 weeks, volume −41–60% exponentially, keep intensity and frequency. DOI 10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0
- Wang et al. 2023, *PLOS ONE* 18(5):e0282838 (14 studies, endurance athletes): time-trial improvement pre→post taper SMD = −0.45 (p < 0.05); 41–60% volume cut with unchanged intensity and frequency was effective across ≤7, 8–14 and 15–21 day durations; adding an overload block before the taper gave a larger TT benefit than taper alone. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0282838
- Aubry 2014 (above) qualifies the overload-before-taper idea: the overload must not tip into functional overreaching.

## 5. Progression models

**Load autoregulation by RPE/RIR**
- Zourdos et al. 2016, *JSCR* 30(1):267–275: RIR-based RPE scale validated against %1RM in experienced and novice squatters; "a practical method to regulate daily training load". DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001049
- Helms, Cronin, Storey & Zourdos 2016, *Strength and Conditioning Journal* 38(4):42–49: application paper for the 1–10 RIR-based scale. DOI 10.1519/SSC.0000000000000218
- Hickmott et al. 2022, *Sports Medicine – Open* 8:9 (18 studies, 15 meta-analysed): RIR/RPE-based loading vs percentage-based, 1RM MD = 3.15 kg (95% CI −0.14 to 6.45), p = 0.06, SMD 0.30; velocity-based vs percentage MD 0.88 kg, p = 0.62; velocity-loss ≤25% gave more strength (MD 2.32 kg, p = 0.02), >25% more hypertrophy. DOI 10.1186/s40798-021-00404-9
- Flexible (autoregulated) vs fixed daily-undulating periodization: McNamara & Stearne 2010 (beginners) and Colquhoun et al. 2017 (trained men, 9 wk) found similar strength gains; a 2024 trial found no added benefit of autoregulation in trained men. Net: RPE-based load progression is at least as good as fixed percentages and costs no testing time. (Sources: Hickmott 2022 above; Colquhoun et al. 2017 *JSCR* 31(2):283–291, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001500; McNamara & Stearne 2010 *JSCR* 24(1):17–22, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181bc177b.)

**Time-based (density) progression vs load progression in circuits**
- No peer-reviewed trial compares density progression (same load, more work per minute) with load progression for functional-fitness or HYROX outcomes. Density training as a method is **coaching lore, not evidence**.
- Indirect support for fixed load, rising volume: Alcaraz 2018 (resisted sled) found load did not determine improvement while a volume threshold did; Blagrove 2018 and Llanos-Lagos 2024 found running economy responds to *high-load* strength work, which is the Strength Session's job, not the HYROX Session's. HIFT periodization trials are small (e.g. a 20-athlete crossover found block loading improved maximal strength without hurting conditioning), with no density-vs-load arm.

**Progressing Station volume at fixed Race load**: nothing published. The practice rests on specificity and on Rappelt 2026's finding that slower athletes pay disproportionately on strength-based Stations, so the Stations must be practised at the weight that will be raced. **Coaching lore, not evidence**.

## 6. Minimum effective Easy-run volume and intensity distribution at 3–5 h/wk

**Volume**
- Thuany et al. 2023, *Science & Sports* (1,091 road runners, cross-sectional): weekly volume was associated with faster pace from 5 km to half marathon; for 5 and 10 km runners higher training frequency also predicted faster pace. Observational. DOI 10.1016/j.scispo.2022.04.005
- Brandt 2025: endurance training volume correlated with HYROX Finish time (ρ = −0.68) in recreational athletes; resistance-training volume did not. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2025.1519240
- McConell et al. 1993, *Int J Sports Med* 14(1):33–37: well-trained runners who cut volume ≈66%, frequency by half and ran everything below 70% VO2max for 4 wk kept VO2max but 9 of 10 ran 5 km slower (16.6 → 16.8 min). Houmard et al. 1990 (DOI 10.1055/s-2007-1024761): a 70% volume cut to 24 km/wk for 3 wk with intensity kept did not change 5 km time or VO2max. Intensity, not easy volume, protects 5 km performance in the short term. DOI 10.1055/s-2007-1021142
- No study defines a minimum Easy-run volume for a 24-min 5K runner. The cross-sectional dose–response (Thuany) and the correlation with HYROX Finish time (Brandt) argue that any added Easy run helps; the detraining studies argue that cutting easy volume for a few weeks costs little if hard sessions stay.

**Distribution at low weekly hours**
- Festa et al. 2020, *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* 1:70 (38 recreational runners, 8 wk, TRIMP-matched): polarised 77/3/20 vs focused 40/50/10 both improved 2 km TT (+3.5% vs +3.0%), vVO2max, thresholds and running economy with no between-group difference; the focused group used 17% less time (24.8 vs 29.9 h total). DOI 10.3389/fspor.2019.00070
- Muñoz 2014 (above): polarised 5.0% vs between-thresholds 3.6% on 10 km in recreational runners, not significant overall. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2012-0350
- Silva Oliveira 2024 (above): polarised vs pyramidal, no difference on VO2peak or time trial; polarised superiority over other distributions appears only in highly trained athletes and short programs. DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z
- Casado et al. 2022, *IJSPP* 17(6):820–833 (systematic review of elite distance runners): pyramidal dominates in preparation phases, shifting toward polarised near competition, within a hard-day/easy-day structure. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435
- Seiler 2010: the 80/20 pattern is described in athletes training 10–13 times/wk; it is a description of elite practice, not a tested optimum for 3–5 h/wk. DOI 10.1123/ijspp.5.3.276
- At 3–5 h/wk with four hard Sessions, the athlete's distribution is necessarily threshold-heavy by time; Festa 2020 is the most relevant trial and found that distribution matters less than matched load at this level.

## Implications for this Program

- **Running is the biggest lever.** Runs are ≈50% of PRO time (Rappelt 2026) and ≈59% in recreational fields (Brandt 2025); VO2max and endurance volume correlate with Finish time, strength measures do not (Brandt 2025). Confirms one dedicated run Session per Week and the optional Easy runs; a run-heavy Phase before HYROX build is justified.
- **Train the fade, not just the pace.** PRO men slow 15% from run 1 to run 8 (HYScience 2026) and the slowest recreational runs follow the sleds (Brandt 2025). HYROX Sessions should pair Stations with the run that follows them, and Race simulations should log per-run splits so the fade is measurable.
- **Row, sandbag lunges, burpee broad jumps and SkiErg deserve the most Station volume**; they best discriminate Finish time among PRO men (Rappelt 2026), and sled pull and lunges carry the largest penalties for slower athletes. Wall balls is where peak HR/lactate/RPE lands (Brandt 2025), so it belongs at the end of Race simulations, not the start.
- **Keep the Threshold Session as long intervals (≥2 min reps, ≥15 min hard work).** This is the dose that beats continuous running on VO2max (Wen 2019; Helgerud 2007: 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax, +7.2%). 1 km repeats qualify; 200 m reps do not.
- **Count the two HYROX Sessions as hard-interval days.** They run at "very hard" HR 80% of the time (Brandt 2025). With the Threshold Session that is three Hard sessions; the Program's rule against consecutive Hard sessions is the right safeguard, and a fourth hard run session is not needed.
- **Strength Session: high load, 2–3 compound lifts, progressed by RPE/RIR.** High-load and combined strength work improve running economy (Llanos-Lagos 2024 ES −0.27/−0.43; Blagrove 2018 2–8%); submaximal-load and isometric work do not. RIR-based loading matches or beats %1RM (Hickmott 2022). Confirms load progression and RPE 7 ≈ 3 RIR in `CONTEXT.md`.
- **Schedule strength ≥3 h from running, lift first if same day, and never the day before the Threshold Session.** Explosive strength is the only outcome concurrent training attenuates and same-session pairing makes it worse (Schumann 2022); resistance-first order protects dynamic strength by ≈7% (Eddens 2018); a lifting bout impairs running for hours to days (Doma 2017).
- **Deload week every 4–6 weeks is defensible but untested.** The coach consensus (Bell 2023) explicitly allows planned or autoregulated deloads; a full week off costs lower-body strength (Coleman 2024). Keep the Program's "reduce by about a third" rule, keep intensity, and let Readiness and Training load trigger an extra Deload week rather than adding fixed ones.
- **Taper: two weeks, cut volume 41–60% exponentially, keep intensity and frequency** (Bosquet 2007; Wang 2023). The current single Taper Week before Race week is at the short end; the data favour starting the volume cut in Week 23 and keeping Station touches short but at Race load. Do not run an overload block into the Taper (Aubry 2014).
- **Three Race simulations are supported by the pacing data**, since no gym-session surrogate reproduces the run-8 fade (HYScience 2026). Their exemption from the 45-min cap is necessary; 60–75 min matches recreational Finish times (Brandt 2025: 86.5 min full race).
- **Easy-run volume: add what fits, cut it first when time is short.** Volume correlates with pace cross-sectionally (Thuany 2023) and with HYROX Finish time (Brandt 2025), but short-term volume cuts with intensity kept do not hurt 5 km time (Houmard 1990; McConell 1993). The optional Easy run is correctly optional.
- **Do not chase a polarised ratio at 3–5 h/wk.** At recreational level, matched-load polarised and threshold-focused plans produce the same gains (Festa 2020; Muñoz 2014 n.s.); polarised vs pyramidal shows no difference (Silva Oliveira 2024). Distribution is a non-issue at this budget; session quality and consistency are the issue.
- **Progression by Station volume then time targets, at fixed Race load, is coaching lore.** Nothing published tests density progression. Keep it, but log it as Training load so the Program can be evaluated after Race day.

## What is not known

- No study links gym strength gains (squat, deadlift, carries) to sled push/pull or farmers-carry Station time in HYROX.
- No study quantifies "compromised running" against the same athlete's open 1 km or 5 km pace, or tests whether training run-after-Station reduces the fade more than separate running and Station work.
- No controlled comparison of density/time-based progression versus load progression in circuit or functional-fitness training.
- No trial of planned versus autoregulated Deload weeks in any population; "every 4th week" is practice, not data.
- No dose–response study of HIIT sessions per week (1 vs 2 vs 3) in recreational runners at a fixed ≤4 sessions/week budget.
- No minimum Easy-run volume established for a ~24-min 5K runner; the recreational evidence is cross-sectional.
- HYROX performance data are PRO/ELITE (Rappelt 2026, HYScience 2026) or n = 11 recreational on a treadmill (Brandt 2025); no peer-reviewed Men's Open or Doubles field analysis exists.
- Taper meta-analyses pool swimmers, cyclists and runners; no taper study covers a hybrid run-plus-station event.
- Doubles-specific demands (alternating Station halves, continuous 8 km running) are unstudied; training at singles volume is a conservative assumption, not an evidenced one.
